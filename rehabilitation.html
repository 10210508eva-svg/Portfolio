Project overview

Rehabilitation requires consistency, but staying motivated can be difficult, especially when patients are exercising at home without direct support from their clinicians.

Our project explores how gamification, movement tracking, and AI-guided exercises can make remote rehabilitation more engaging and accessible. We are developing a patient app and a clinician dashboard that connect both sides, helping patients stay motivated while giving clinicians a better way to monitor progress and communicate with patients.

I joined the ASU Human-In-Mind Engineering Lab as a summer research intern and continued working remotely during the school year. My main responsibility is designing and prototyping both interfaces, from early research and information architecture to wireframes and UI design.

Problem

Staying motivated during rehabilitation is difficult

Repetitive exercises can become boring, especially when patients need to complete them independently at home.

Patients need a clearer way to understand their progress

Without accessible progress reports and feedback, it can be difficult for patients to see how their exercises are helping.

Communication between patients and clinicians can be limited

Clinicians need an easier way to monitor exercise progress and stay connected with patients between appointments.

01

Background research & literature review

I started by reviewing research papers about game-based rehabilitation, patient motivation, and EMG technology. I focused on understanding the methods used in previous studies and how they could help us design a better rehabilitation experience.

I also looked into the needs of patients and clinicians, which helped me think about what information and features should be included in each interface.

Key takeaways

  • Motivation should be considered throughout the patient experience, not just during exercises.

  • Patients and clinicians need different types of information, so both interfaces should be designed around their own goals.

  • Research helped me make more thoughtful decisions about what features to include and how to organize them.

02

Competitive analysis

I explored existing rehabilitation platforms to understand how they organize their features and present information to patients and clinicians. I paid extra attention to the home page and progress reports, since I wanted to make sure users could quickly find the information they needed.

Medbridge was one of my main references, but I also compared other platforms to explore different approaches to rehabilitation, tracking, and patient management.

  • I wanted our app to feel easy to start using without overwhelming patients with too many features.

03

User needs & feature definition

After reviewing the research and existing applications, I started defining what patients and clinicians would need from our platform.

Even though they are using the same system, their goals are different. Patients need an experience that encourages them to keep exercising, while clinicians need a clear way to manage patients and understand their progress.

Patient

  • Complete rehabilitation exercises

  • Track exercise streaks and progress

  • View badges and anonymous leaderboards

  • Message their clinician

  • Review rehabilitation reports

Clinician

  • View patient roster and summaries

  • Track exercise progress and trends

  • Set exercise goals

  • Review patient notes and records

  • Communicate with patients

Key takeaways

  • Patients need motivation and an easy way to access exercises.

  • Clinicians need organized information that helps them manage multiple patients.

  • Both interfaces should feel connected while supporting different workflows.

04

Information architecture

One of my biggest challenges was figuring out how to organize all the features without making the app too complicated.

I created separate information architectures for patients and clinicians. I tried to think from each user's perspective and imagine what they would want to access first.

I had a lot of ideas at the beginning, but I wanted to keep the navigation simple. I eventually organized the main features into four tabs for each interface, focusing on the information users would need most often.

Key takeaways

  • Having more features doesn't always mean a better experience.

  • I needed to prioritize the most important information instead of giving everything the same level of attention.

  • Keeping both interfaces to four main tabs helped me build a clearer navigation structure.

05

Low-fidelity wireframes

I started with the patient's home page because I felt it was one of the most important parts of the app. I wanted to make sure the main features were easy to find and that the rest of the experience could connect naturally to it.

Throughout the process, I received a lot of feedback from my PhD mentor, Hanna, especially on the patient's home page. I made several changes based on our discussions and continued refining both interfaces.

I also wanted to give equal attention to the patient and clinician sides. Even though their needs are different, I didn't want either interface to feel less complete than the other.

✎ My thinking

One thing I kept thinking about during this project was how to make the app useful without making it too complicated.

I had a lot of ideas for both interfaces, but I realized that I couldn't put everything on the home page. I needed to think about what users would actually want to see first and what information could be placed somewhere else.

I'm especially proud of the clinician's home page because I came up with the layout and organization myself. I wanted clinicians to have a clear overview of their patients and be able to access more detailed information when needed.

Design challenge

Supporting accurate rehabilitation at home

One of the biggest challenges of remote rehabilitation is that patients may struggle to perform exercises correctly without a clinician guiding them in person. This can make it difficult for patients to know whether they are doing the movements properly.

To address this, our research team is exploring the use of XREAL smart glasses and MediaPipe hand tracking to track patients' movements and provide more accurate, real-time feedback during rehabilitation exercises. We hope this technology can help patients exercise more confidently and independently at home, while allowing clinicians to better understand their progress.

My focus

For my design work, I focused on connecting these exercises with the patient app, making it easy for patients to access their exercises, view their results, and stay motivated throughout their rehabilitation journey.

Key learnings

What I’d carry forward

What I learned

This project helped me understand how different users can have completely different needs, even when they are using the same platform. I learned that good design isn't just about making an interface look nice, but also about understanding what information people actually need.

What was hard

The biggest challenge was trying to meet everyone's expectations while keeping the design simple and easy to use. I received a lot of feedback throughout the process, and sometimes it was difficult to decide which ideas should be prioritized.

Next steps →

We are continuing to develop the project and preparing for usability testing. I'm also involved in planning the questionnaires and surveys. I'm looking forward to seeing how real users interact with the app and using their feedback to improve the design.

Patient app

Rehabilitation That Keeps You Going

Onboarding

Patients share their dominant hand and affected side, so the first plan fits.

Home

Today’s goals, hand scores and the latest clinician note, all on the first screen. I explored two home layouts: data-first and coach-first.

✎ Design decision

I decided to focus more on showing patients their progress and keeping them motivated. I wanted patients to see how much they’ve improved as soon as they open the app, instead of having to look through different pages. I also included a weekly streak tracker to encourage them to keep up with their exercises and build a habit of doing rehabilitation regularly.

Training

Every exercise opens with a demo video, its benefits and step-by-step instructions. Patients choose Recommended (the clinician’s plan) or Practice (their own goal).

Report

Reports turn grip scores into trends against a target range. Streaks and badges reward showing up, and reports can be shared with the care team in one tap.

Profile

Recovery stats live in the profile, along with pairing the XREAL glasses.

Clinician app

Who needs attention today?

Onboarding

Clinicians add their licence, job title and clinic, and are verified before any patient data is shared.

Home

A roster sorted by who’s falling behind. Each patient opens into an overview, trends, private notes and their full record.

Message

One thread per patient, with quick replies for common answers and a plan builder for level and targets.

Insight

Goal completion, streaks and weekly compliance across every patient.

Profile

Licence details, an audit log and clinical obligations in one place.
